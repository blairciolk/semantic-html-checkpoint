Introduction to the Sun

The Sun is the star at the center of our Solar System. It provides Earth with the light and heat needed for life. The Sun is much larger than Earth, and all eight planets travel around it. Although it looks small from Earth, the Sun is actually about 93 million miles away.

What Is the Sun?

The Sun is a huge ball of extremely hot gas. It is made mostly of hydrogen and helium. The Sun produces a huge amount of energy, which gives off the light and heat we experience on Earth.

How Big is the Sun?

The Sun is much larger than Earth. About 109 Earths could fit across the Sun. Its size also allows it to make up most of the mass in the Solar System. Even though the Sun is enormous, it is still just one of billions of stars in space.

The Sun's Temperature

The Sun is extremely hot. Its surface has a temperature of about 10,000°F (5,500°C). The inside of the Sun is much hotter. This extreme heat allows the Sun to produce the energy that reaches Earth as light and heat.

The Sun and Earth

The Sun is very important to Earth. Its light helps plants grow, while its heat keeps Earth warm enough for life. Earth is about 93 million miles from the Sun, and sunlight takes about eight minutes to reach us.

How Old is the Sun?

The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old. It has been shining for billions of years and is expected to continue producing light and heat for billions more. The Sun has played an important role in the Solar System since it formed.

Why is the Sun Important?

Without the Sun, Earth would be dark and extremely cold. Plants would not be able to grow, and life as we know it could not exist. The Sun also provides the gravity that keeps the planets moving around it.

Additional Information

  • Diameter: 864,000 miles
  • Distance from Earth: 93 million miles
  • Luminosity: 3.83 x 10²⁶ watts
  • Surface Temperature: 5,500°C
  • Color: White
  • Age: 4.6 billion years
  • Composition: Hydrogen and helium
  • Star Type: G-type main-sequence star
